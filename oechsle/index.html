How it’s worked out

SG and Brix

°Brix = ((182.46007 × SG − 775.68212) × SG + 1262.7794) × SG − 669.56218, a fit to the NBS sugar tables accurate to 0.002 °Bx up to SG 1.179. The reverse is solved exactly. The “gravity points ÷ 4” shortcut is close at 1.040 but 0.67 °Bx too high at 1.080 and 1.9 too high at 1.120.

Plato, Balling and Blg

Plato uses Buhl’s fit to the brewing tables. Brix, Balling (Blg in Poland) and Plato are the same idea, sugar as a percentage of mass, and differ by less than 0.02 between 1.000 and 1.120.

Oechsle, Babo and Baumé

°Oechsle = 1000 × (SG − 1). Babo (KMW, used in Austria and Italy) relates to it as °Oe = KMW × (4.54 + 0.022 × KMW). Baumé is the heavy scale with modulus 145: °Bé = 145 × (1 − 1/SG).

Potential alcohol

Grape must holds non-sugar solids, so the same gravity means less sugar than in a honey or sugar must. The grape column uses OIV Table II (from 1.041; below that the table has no rows). The honey column uses honey’s sugar content.
